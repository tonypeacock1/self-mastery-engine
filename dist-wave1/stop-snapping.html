How to Stop Snapping at People

You do not plan to snap. It arrives larger than the moment — a short email, a slow queue at the robot, a child asking the same question twice. Afterwards there is the familiar drop: that was out of proportion. Understanding why is more useful than another lecture on patience.

Short temper under fatigue is one of the most common complaints among able people who still care about how they treat others. The shame after the fact is often worse than the incident. Willpower helps for a week. Then tiredness returns and the threshold drops again.

Overreaction is information

When the response is bigger than the stimulus, something older or accumulated is riding along with the present moment. That is not an excuse. It is a diagnostic clue. People who want composure are usually willing to examine that clue if they are given a clear frame instead of moralising.

International advice tends to recycle breathing exercises and communication scripts. Those are fine tactics. They do not explain the charge that makes the tactic fail when the day has already spent you.

Composure under South African load

Traffic, crime-adjacent vigilance, service delivery friction, workplace intensity — the background hum is higher than wellness copy admits. Building composure here means working with real baseline stress, not a quiet suburb in a different country. Local recognition is not decoration. It is accuracy.

FAQ

Why do I overreact to small things?

Small things land on a system that is already loaded. The visible trigger is not the full cause. Residual stress reactions lower the threshold so ordinary friction produces an oversized response.

How do I stop snapping at people?

Short-term: notice the early body signal and create a pause. Longer-term: examine and reduce the stored charge that makes the pause impossible when you are tired. Scripts without capacity work tend to collapse under fatigue.

Why am I more reactive when tired?

Fatigue removes the buffer that normally keeps residual patterns in check. The pattern was already there; tiredness removes the cover.

How do I keep composure under pressure?

Composure is partly skill and partly available capacity. Training the skill while ignoring capacity is why people “know better” and still snap at 18:40.
